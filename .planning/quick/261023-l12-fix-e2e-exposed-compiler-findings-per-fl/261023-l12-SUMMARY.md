## Matmul coverage heads-up — outcome: NEGATIVE, with evidence

The heads-up predicted that C1's flat-rank row ownership plus a thread-split strip would leave a large residual canary (~94% predicted) on matmul even after C1b, and prescribed the slow-coordinate fix (init=index(0), stride=realized thread extent, trips=T/stride) only on confirming evidence.

**Measured evidence (e2e run after the reducer rework): matmul canary = 0, checksum bitwise-exact, all 8 samples exact — twice consecutively, plus memcheck/racecheck clean.**

Root-cause of the negative outcome, from the regenerated golden (`internal/cg/cxx/testdata/matmul_store.cu`): the C1b forced-FRAG tier changed the searched optimum so that the predicted shape no longer exists. The C1b optimum assigns **N and K sequential rounds** (rhs declined from the warp tier by F6 `ReasonFragBytes` — FRAG bytes exceed MaxFragPerThread — so no thread dim ever splits N), and the ONLY thread-coupled loop is M's inside walk (`ThrIterator` init `thread_map1.index()` — the flat rank — step 256, bound = the m tile). Coverage is complete by construction: thread t walks rows {t, t+256, …} ∩ tile — exactly one in-bounds row per thread at T=64 — fills its own `Frag` with `lhs[base+t, k-strip]`, and writes `out[base+t, n, k]` for every (n, k) via the Seq walks. The predicted "row tid AND strip tid" diagonal coupling required a thread-parallel N between-rounds loop, which the C1b search product does not contain. The concern applied to the pre-C1b shape; it is structurally absent in the shipped one. No fix landed; no gate conditioning needed.
